# Social insect work organisation and leaderless task allocation in robot and UAV swarms

Scope note: notes support judging a proposed design with three roles (scout finds and reports a POI, catcher acts on it, backup waits), an acknowledged auction for allocation today, and per-UAV local decisions on shared information. Civilian scope; military programmes are recorded only for their allocation and human-supervision architecture. Most search hits were abstracts, press releases or secondary summaries; where only a secondary source was available this is flagged.

## Honeybees: scouts, waggle-dance recruitment, quorum sensing, stop signals, error and stale-information handling

### Takeaway
A bee swarm picks a nest site without a leader by combining positive feedback (scouts dance for sites in proportion to quality and recruit others), built-in decay of advocacy (dancers dance less on each return and then stop, so stale or weak support "expires"), negative feedback (stop signals aimed at rival-site dancers and at dangerous food sources), and a local quorum test at the site (about 15-30 scouts present at once) rather than global consensus. The quorum threshold explicitly trades speed against accuracy, and the system still makes occasional errors (split decisions, local optima).

### Cited Findings
- Seeley and Visscher compared "consensus sensing" (scouts wait until all dancers advertise one site) with "quorum sensing" (scouts detect enough bees at one site); scouts ignore whether consensus exists and track whether a quorum has formed at a candidate site — [Cornell Chronicle summary of Seeley's work](https://news.cornell.edu/index%2ephp/node/276432); 2004 paper: Seeley & Visscher, "Quorum sensing during nest-site selection by honeybee swarms", Behav Ecol Sociobiol 56:594-601 — [Seeley & Visscher 2004 PDF (UCR)](https://bees.ucr.edu/media/166/download)
- A quorum is reached when about 20-30 bees are at one potential nest at the same time (secondary account of Seeley's results) — [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/index%2ephp/node/276432); another secondary account says a quorum "can be as few as 15 scout bees out of a swarm of 4,000" — [American Bee Journal 2017](https://americanbeejournal.mydigitalpublication.com/june-2017-vol-157-no-6/page-622?pp=1)
- Passino & Seeley (2006) built a model that reproduces the empirically observed decision process and used it to study the speed-accuracy trade-off; lowering the quorum to 15 bees gave quick but error-prone decisions, raising it above 20-30 gave slower decisions that were only slightly more accurate — [ScienceDaily 2006 summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2006/04/060420233908.htm); [Passino & Seeley 2006 PDF](https://www.shoalsmarinelaboratory.org/sites/default/files/media/2026-02/Passino%26Seeley%202006_SML127.pdf)
- The swarm's strategy is described as a "weighted additive" strategy (most accurate, most information-demanding), made workable by distributing both search and evaluation across many bees — [ScienceDaily 2006](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2006/04/060420233908.htm)
- "Expiration of dissent": Seeley (2003) "Consensus building during nest-site selection in honey bee swarms: the expiration of dissent", Behav Ecol Sociobiol — [DOI 10.1007/s00265-003-0598-z](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/S00265-003-0598-Z). Secondary account: nest-site scouts have a high drop-out rate from dancing, quitting faster than nectar foragers, for both good and poor sites; this fading of dances for weaker sites is central to reaching a quorum — [American Bee Journal 2017](https://americanbeejournal.mydigitalpublication.com/june-2017-vol-157-no-6/page-622?pp=1)
- One observed swarm (July 1997) had about 16 hours of dancing over three days; early on scouts advertised all 11 candidate sites with no clear leader, then one site gradually came to dominate and was chosen — [American Bee Journal 2017](https://americanbeejournal.mydigitalpublication.com/june-2017-vol-157-no-6/page-622?pp=1)
- Seeley & Visscher's 2004 Apidologie review covers how scouts sense that group decision making is ending so they can start rousing the swarm for departure — [Apidologie 2004](https://www.apidologie.org/articles/apido/abs/2004/02/M4204/M4204.html)
- Stop signals in house hunting (Seeley, Visscher, Schlegel, Hogan, Franks & Marshall, Science 335(6064):108-111; online Dec 2011, print 2012): a stop signal is a brief vibration delivered with a head butt to a dancer; scouts committed to one site aim stop signals mainly at dancers for the rival site (cross inhibition); inhibition strength scales with group size; this breaks deadlock between equal sites; repeated signals raise the probability a dancer stops; once one site had roughly 50-100 dancers, scouts began head-butting any remaining dancer; first demonstration of stop signals in house hunting — [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/node/271186); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/12/111208141942.htm)
- Modelling (Pais et al. 2013, PLoS ONE): the stop-signal mechanism lets swarms break deadlock randomly between equal sites and converge on the better site when the quality difference is large enough — [PMC3759446](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3759446)
- Modelling (Laomettachit et al. 2016): stop-signalling reduces split decisions and improves speed, but with high quorum thresholds it can compromise accuracy by trapping the swarm in local optima — [KMUTT record](https://kirim.kmutt.ac.th/converis/portal/detail/Publication/600978840?lang=en_GB)
- Lindauer's 1955 records: 2 of 19 swarms lifted off while dancing had not converged on one site (excerpt truncated; consensus is not required for departure) — [American Bee Journal 2017](https://americanbeejournal.mydigitalpublication.com/june-2017-vol-157-no-6/page-622?pp=1)
- Stop signals in foraging (Nieh 2010, Current Biology 20:310-315): the waggle dance is positive feedback; foragers attacked at a food source raised stop-signal output about 43-fold, simulated biting 88-fold, alarm pheromone 14-fold; attackers and unharmed foragers did not change output; signals were targeted at nestmates visiting the same source (identified by odour); described as first clear evidence of negative feedback triggered by foraging peril — [Nieh 2010 PDF](https://labs.biology.ucsd.edu/nieh/papers/NiehCurrBio2010.pdf); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/02/100211121800.htm)
- Companion agent-based model (Johnson & Nieh 2010): the stop signal counterbalances the dance and lets the colony reallocate foragers away from endangered sources, increasing exploitation efficiency — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/02/100211121800.htm)

### Inferences
- Bee mapping to the proposed roles: scout = nest-site scout (finds, evaluates, advertises); catcher = recruited bee that commits; backup = uncommitted swarm members. The bee mechanism differs from an auction in that commitment is gated by an independent local check (a quorum of bees physically at the site), not by one scout's report.
- "Stale information" is handled structurally, not by explicit timestamps: advocacy decays automatically with each re-report (dance attrition) unless refreshed by re-visits. A UAV analogue is a scout report with a decaying weight or time-to-live that must be refreshed by re-observation, so an unrefreshed report stops recruiting by itself.
- Negative feedback has two distinct uses worth copying: (a) cross-inhibition between competing options to avoid deadlock/split commitment, and (b) a peril-triggered abort aimed only at those committed to the affected option (Nieh). For a small fleet the second maps to a targeted "abort/stop" message from a catcher that finds the POI invalid or unsafe.
- The quorum threshold is a tunable speed-accuracy dial with diminishing returns above a point; in a 3-10 UAV fleet a "quorum" can only be very small (e.g. scout report plus one independent confirmation, or plus operator confirmation), so the dial is coarse.

### Gaps
- Full texts of Seeley & Visscher 2003/2004 and Seeley 2003 were not retrieved; exact quorum counts and dance-attrition rates come from secondary sources.
- Proportion of a swarm acting as scouts and the piping/departure signal mechanics were not sourced in this pass.
- No source found that quantifies how often real swarms choose a worse site or split (error rates in the field).

## Ants: stigmergy, pheromone trails, response thresholds, task switching, reserve workers

### Takeaway
Ant colonies allocate work through local cues: the stimulus a task emits, individual response thresholds that may adapt with experience, the rate of encounters with nestmates doing other tasks, and pheromone trails (stigmergy). A large share of workers is idle at any time, and experiments show these "lazy" workers act as reserve labour that steps in when highly active workers are lost, driven by task urgency rather than a goal of keeping everyone busy.

### Cited Findings
- Fixed response-threshold model (Bonabeau, Theraulaz & Deneubourg 1996, Proc R Soc B 263:1565-1569): individuals respond to task-related stimuli with response thresholds; when stimulus intensity exceeds an individual's threshold the individual performs the task with high probability, and successful performance reduces the stimulus — [Bonabeau et al. 1996 record](https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/111795)
- Companion paper (Bull Math Biol 1998) shows the fixed-threshold model accounts for Wilson's (1984) experimental observations (workers of one caste taking over when the other is removed) and explores extensions including temporal polyethism — [DOI 10.1006/bulm.1998.0041](https://www.doi.org/10.1006/BULM.1998.0041)
- Variable-threshold (reinforcement) model (Theraulaz, Bonabeau & Deneubourg 1998): performing a task lowers the threshold for it and not performing it raises it, so specialists emerge from initially identical individuals; the authors note fixed-threshold models assume pre-differentiated individuals and cannot explain the genesis of task allocation — [Proc R Soc B 1998 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1688885)
- The two most-used division-of-labour models are the deterministic threshold model (DTM, Page & Mitchell 1998: each worker does the task with the largest positive stimulus-minus-threshold) and the probabilistic threshold model (PTM, Bonabeau et al. 1996) — [Lichocki et al. 2012 (EPFL)](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/182822/files/lichocki12b.pdf?version=1)
- Field application: in the ponerine ant Ectatomma ruidum, a threshold framework distinguished hunters subdivided into stingers and transporters — [Schatz et al. abstract](https://iridia.ulb.ac.be/ants98/ABSTRACTS/schatz.html)
- Gordon: colony organisation emerges from local interactions; no ant assesses colony needs; the rate of brief encounters, rather than information content, influences task decisions; a harvester ant's task depends on the rate at which it meets workers of other tasks — [Gordon lab research page](https://web.stanford.edu/~dmgordon/old2/research.html); 2007 paper "Interaction rate informs harvester ant task decisions" (Behavioral Ecology) listed there
- Harvester ant patrollers scout the foraging area each morning and, on return, stimulate foragers to emerge; returning patroller-specific cuticular hydrocarbons were sufficient to restore foraging after suppression (conference abstract, preliminary) — [Greene & Gordon, SICB abstract](https://sicb.org/?p=34412)
- Colonies regulate foraging with feedback from the rate at which inactive ants inside the nest encounter returning successful foragers — [Gordon lab research page](https://web.stanford.edu/~dmgordon/old2/research.html)
- Reserve workers (Charbonneau, Sasaki & Dornhaus 2017, PLOS ONE, Temnothorax rugatulus): around 40% of workers in lab colonies were mostly inactive; after the most active 20% were removed, mostly inactive workers increased activity and replaced the lost labour within about a week; removing the least active 20% produced no replacement; replacement tracked the urgency of the task (foraging, brood care) rather than a target level of overall colony activity — [DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0184074](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0184074); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/09/170908205356.htm)
- Inactive workers had more swollen abdomens (possible food stores); support for a reserve role in other insects is reported as weak; alternative explanations (seasonal tasks, age) remain open — [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/09/170908205356.htm)
- Pheromone trails as stigmergy: army ants lay and maintain pheromone paths between nest and food; EPFL used this as the model for fixed-wing MAV swarms where pheromone is "virtually deposited" on the aircraft via local communication — [Hauert et al. 2008, Swarm Intelligence (EPFL PDF)](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/124898/files/SI_final.pdf?version=1)
- Stigmergy definition as used in UAV work: coordination by modifying a shared environment instead of direct messaging; digital pheromones deposited where a drone senses a possible target accumulate, diffuse to neighbours and evaporate over time, forming an attractive field — [Univ. Pisa repository](https://arpi.unipi.it/handle/11568/752393)

### Inferences
- The "backup" role in the proposed design closely matches ant reserve labour. The ant evidence suggests backups should be activated by an uncovered urgent task (e.g. a catcher lost or an unserved POI) rather than kept busy, and that only loss of active members should trigger backfill.
- A response-threshold rule is a natural local fallback for backups: stimulus = how long/how urgently a reported POI has gone unserved; heterogeneous thresholds (fixed per UAV) stagger responses so not all backups launch at once. A deterministic threshold (DTM-style) with fixed tie-break order is easier to verify than the probabilistic PTM.
- Gordon's encounter-rate mechanism shows a colony can sense load from message rates alone; in a UAV fleet the analogue is using heartbeat/report rates as stimuli, but this is less precise than explicit state sharing that a 3-10 UAV fleet can afford.
- Variable thresholds create specialists over time; in a small fleet this could make role assignment history-dependent and harder to predict, which argues for fixed thresholds in safety-relevant paths.

### Gaps
- Full texts of Bonabeau 1996 and Theraulaz 1998 not retrieved; equation forms (e.g. s^n/(s^n+theta^n)) not verified here.
- No source retrieved on ant error correction (e.g. trail-following errors, pheromone evaporation rates as "forgetting") beyond the general stigmergy description.

## Leaderless task allocation: auctions (CBAA/CBBA/contract net) vs response thresholds vs stigmergy

### Takeaway
Market methods give the best allocations when information is accurate and have provable properties (CBBA: conflict-free convergence and at least 50% of optimal under a diminishing-marginal-gain scoring condition), but their guarantees rest on assumptions (connected network, synchronous rounds) that break on lossy links, where duplicate or missing assignments can occur. Threshold methods are cheaper and match market quality when information is inaccurate. Stigmergy suits coverage and search, not exclusive assignment. Robot-swarm studies show ant-like recruitment improves performance on clustered tasks but interference grows with group size.

### Cited Findings
- Taxonomy (Gerkey & Matarić 2004, IJRR 23(9):939-954): domain-independent multi-robot task allocation taxonomy on three axes: single-task vs multi-task robots (ST/MT), single-robot vs multi-robot tasks (SR/MR), instantaneous vs time-extended assignment (IA/TA); many MRTA problems map onto known optimisation problems — [Gerkey & Matarić 2004 PDF](https://cse-robotics.engr.tamu.edu/dshell/cs689/papers/gerkey04formal.pdf); ST-SR-IA is an instance of the optimal assignment problem — [arXiv 2105.13108 (secondary)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.13108)
- Contract Net (Smith 1980, IEEE Trans Computers C-29(12):1104-1113): task distribution by negotiation between nodes holding tasks and nodes able to execute them, with no central control or data; demonstrated on a distributed sensing problem; claimed to avoid bottlenecks by distributing control — [Smith 1980 PDF](https://www.cs.ucf.edu/~lboloni/Teaching/EEL6788_2008/papers/The_Contract_Net_Protocol_Dec-1980.pdf)
- CBAA/CBBA (Choi, Brunet & How 2009, IEEE T-RO 25(4):912-926): CBAA is a single-assignment auction, CBBA its multi-assignment (bundle) extension; agents bid, then a local-communication consensus phase agrees on winning bids; both converge to a conflict-free allocation with a worst-case performance guarantee when scoring satisfies diminishing marginal gain; they produce conflict-free plans even with inconsistent situational awareness, because consensus is on winning bids rather than on a shared world picture — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/52330); [MIT ACL CBBA page](https://acl.mit.edu/projects/consensus-based-bundle-algorithm)
- CBBA worst-case bound: converged score at least 50% of optimal; original proof assumes a static connected network and synchronised consensus rounds (secondary summary) — [MIT ACL CBBA page](https://acl.mit.edu/projects/consensus-based-bundle-algorithm); [KAIST record](https://dspace.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/19807)
- Alternative approaches first make situational awareness consistent across the fleet, which can take long and need large data transfers (contrast drawn by CBBA authors, secondary) — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/52330)
- ACBBA (Johnson, Ponda, Choi & How 2010/2011): CBBA relies on global synchronisation that is hard to enforce; running CBBA unchanged asynchronously makes agents rebroadcast full state and swamp the network; ACBBA uses local deconfliction rules, handles out-of-order messages and redundant information, preserves convergence properties with less communication, and adds an asynchronous replan for large changes in local situational awareness — [ACBBA paper PDF (MIT)](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/1721.1/81434/1/How_Asynchronous%20decentralized.pdf)
- On realistic lossy networks ACBBA deviated from its theoretical behaviour, especially as agents and tasks increased, producing the same task assigned to multiple agents and/or tasks left unassigned (Rantanen et al. 2018; presentation slides, not peer reviewed) — [SigPort](https://sigport.org/node/3370)
- Market vs threshold (Kalra & Martinoli, DARS 2006): with accurate information market-based allocation is more efficient; with inaccurate information threshold-based allocation reaches the same quality at a fraction of the cost; both robust to small communication and perception ranges; market methods "exploit points of centralization in the form of auctions"; threshold methods use less communication and computation — [Kalra & Martinoli 2006 PDF (EPFL)](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/83462/files/NK_DARS06.pdf?version=2); [CMU RI record](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/a-comparative-study-of-market-based-and-threshold-based-task-allocation)
- Ant-like recruitment in robots (Krieger, Billeter & Keller 2000, Nature 406:992-995): groups with ant-inspired decentralised control foraged more efficiently than single robots; the advantage shrank in larger groups, likely due to interference; with clustered food, groups that recruited each other outperformed groups without information transfer — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/35023164)
- Honeybee-derived decision design pattern (Reina, Valentini, Fernández-Oto, Dorigo & Trianni 2015, PLoS ONE): formal guidelines to implement collective decisions at the individual level so outcomes quantitatively match macroscopic model predictions; covers homogeneous and heterogeneous agents — [ISTC-CNR](https://istc.cnr.it/en/node/75448); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0140950.html)
- Robot implementation of bee-like decisions (March-Pons et al. 2024, Phys Rev Research): up to 35 Kilobots ran a nest-site-selection model combining self-discovery and imitation; consensus without central control, with strength depending on density and motion (percolated communication network) — [arXiv 2310.15592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15592)
- Stigmergic UAV coverage: SAGA (ECHORD++) UAV swarm discretises a field into a grid; each UAV chooses the next cell probabilistically (random walk) weighting its own and peers' influence, sharing positions and visited cells; flies low only where there is evidence of weeds; two phases (low-resolution coverage, then focus) — [ERCIM News (Albani & Trianni)](https://ercim-news.ercim.eu/en113/special/drone-swarms-in-the-field)
- Digital-pheromone control (Sauter et al. 2008, SPIE): onboard pheromone logic on each vehicle self-organised surveillance, detection, sensor cueing and recognition with no central processing and minimal operator input; adaptive logic absorbed some unplanned hardware failures without human intervention — [SoarTech/SPIE 2008](https://soartech.com/knowledge_center/distributed-pheromone-based-swarming-control-of-unmanned-air-and-ground-vehicles-for-rsta/)
- Swarm engineering perspective: review classifies work by design/analysis method and by collective behaviour and identifies gaps for swarm robotics as an engineering discipline — [Brambilla et al. 2013, Swarm Intelligence (DOI)](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/S11721-012-0075-2)
- Aerial swarm survey (Chung et al. 2018, IEEE T-RO) lists task allocation as one of six core areas alongside trajectory generation, adversarial control, distributed sensing, monitoring and mapping — [Caltech record](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/87925/)

### Inferences
- Comparison for the proposed design (scout/catcher/backup, 3-10 UAVs):
  - Auction (CBAA-style single assignment fits ST-SR-IA: each catcher serves one POI at a time). Strengths: near-optimal with accurate information, explicit ownership, conflict-freedom provable under stated assumptions, deterministic given identical inputs and fixed tie-breaks. Failure modes: assumptions (connectivity, synchronised rounds) break under packet loss; duplicates or orphaned tasks on lossy links; dead winners hold tasks unless ownership expires; re-auctions can oscillate if scores change faster than consensus converges. Scales poorly in messages for large swarms but trivially at N≤10.
  - Response thresholds. Strengths: minimal communication, graceful under noisy information, natural backfill (Wilson/Charbonneau behaviour). Failure modes: no exclusivity guarantee (two UAVs can cross threshold together), probabilistic variants are not deterministic, outcomes verified statistically by simulation/macro models rather than by proof.
  - Stigmergy. Strengths: excellent for coverage/search/monitoring (survey, spraying, fire detection), tolerant of agent loss. Failure modes: needs a shared "environment" (map) that in UAVs is itself communicated; evaporation/diffusion parameters are tuning-sensitive; does not assign exclusive POIs.
- Hybrid most consistent with evidence: auction for exclusive POI ownership when the link is good; time-decaying scout reports (bee dance attrition) as the input to the auction; deterministic response thresholds as the degraded-mode fallback for backups when acknowledgements stop; stigmergic shared coverage maps for the scouting/search phase.
- Because N≤10 makes full state sharing cheap, an alternative to bid-consensus is "consensus on inputs, identical deterministic solve at every node" (all UAVs run the same optimal-assignment solver on the same acked snapshot). This trades CBBA's tolerance of inconsistent awareness for easier verification; it needs explicit snapshot/version agreement.

### Gaps
- No primary paper retrieved that measures oscillation/churning of CBBA re-planning in flight; Rantanen et al. lossy-link result is from slides.
- Exact efficiency/cost numbers from Kalra & Martinoli 2006 not extracted.
- Khamis et al. (2015) MRTA review and Gerkey & Matarić's MURDOCH auction work were not retrieved.
- No head-to-head study found comparing auction vs threshold vs stigmergy on fixed-wing UAVs specifically.

## Real deployments: which UAV swarm programmes and products used which allocation method, and what worked in the field

### Takeaway
Publicly documented outdoor UAV swarm results mostly demonstrate decentralised flocking, leader-follower behaviour, or supervised multi-vehicle teaming; detailed allocation algorithms of military programmes and commercial products are not public. Bio-inspired allocation has field evidence mainly from one digital-pheromone air-ground demonstration and from small-scale or lab robot swarms. Every fielded programme found keeps a human operator supervising, usually at a ratio of one or two operators to many vehicles. Reviews still describe moving swarm robotics into practice as unsolved.

### Cited Findings
- Vásárhelyi et al. 2018 (Science Robotics 3:eaat3536): 30 drones, self-organised flocking with collision and obstacle avoidance, no central control; described as the largest such outdoor system to date; model parameters tuned by evolutionary optimisation because delays, limited communication and obstacles must be modelled explicitly; 30 drones flew at 8 m/s in confined spaces bounded by virtual GPS walls; 1000-drone simulation as scalability evidence; no task allocation — [ELTE project page](https://hal.elte.hu/drones/scirob2018.html); [VU research record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/optimized-flocking-of-autonomous-drones-in-confined-environments/)
- NPS ARSENL (2015): 50 off-the-shelf fixed-wing UAVs flown simultaneously at Camp Roberts as two 25-vehicle sub-swarms, controlled by two operators managing behaviours and health; behaviours were basic leader-follower with wireless information exchange; build-up 6 → 9 → 12 → 20 → 30 → 50 aircraft over about nine months — [US Navy press release](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/Press-Releases/display-pressreleases/Article/2263000/arsenl-reaches-its-ultimate-goal-of-50-autonomous-uavs-in-flight/); a safety pilot was on standby — [NPR 2015](https://www.npr.org/sections/alltechconsidered/2015/07/23/424685529/follow-the-leader-drones-learn-to-behave-in-swarms)
- A later NPS thesis (Ventresca, 2025) proposes Dynamic Stable Matching as an alternative to auction- and forest-based allocation for UAV swarm area search; whether flight-tested is not stated in the record — [NPS Calhoun](https://calhoun.nps.edu/entities/publication/bf6de5f2-2cf1-4579-b12e-3ea37e239ca3)
- DARPA OFFSET (2017-2021): six field experiments of increasing complexity; goal of small units controlling up to 250 heterogeneous COTS air and ground robots in urban terrain, focused on swarm autonomy and human-swarm teaming; integrators built swarm architectures with immersive interfaces; final experiment Nov 2021 at Fort Campbell — [DARPA news 2021](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2021/offset-swarms-take-flight); vehicle count reported inconsistently (over 100 vs hundreds) — [Overt Defense](https://overtdefense.com/2021/12/16/darpa-runs-final-test-for-offset-program-drone-swarm); [ExecutiveGov](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/darpa-concludes-final-drone-swarm-control-tech-experiment)
- Perdix (DoD SCO, Oct 2016): 103 micro-drones demonstrated "collective decision-making, adaptive formation flying, and self-healing"; described as sharing one distributed brain with no leader, tolerating drones joining or leaving; algorithms not public — [US INDOPACOM release](https://www.pacom.mil/Media/News/News-Articles/Article/1046043/department-of-defense-announces-successful-micro-drone-demonstration/)
- Shield AI Hivemind: 2023 AFWERX milestone with three V-BATs monitoring simulated wildfires in coordinated Detect, Identify, Locate, Report missions — [sUAS News 2023](https://www.suasnews.com/2023/08/shield-ai-demonstrates-ai-piloted-teaming-v-bats-with-afwerx/); Aug 2026 Taiwan NCSIST exercise: three drones flew a coordinated autonomous search, one agent re-routed around a partial no-fly zone in its sector, autonomous landing after mission; architecture is modular, behaviours composed from reusable "blocks" for single- or multi-agent missions; framed as a minimal operator cadre commanding multiple UAS; company-sourced, allocation method not disclosed — [Shield AI release 2026](https://shield.ai/shield-ai-and-taiwans-ncsist-complete-autonomous-swarm-exercise-and-expand-sovereign-ai-and-autonomy-efforts/); [Shield AI 2022](https://shield.ai/hivemind-for-operational-read-and-react-swarming/)
- Auterion Nemyx (launched Sep 2025; a military strike product, weapon details omitted): publicly described split is that operators select objectives while the engine handles timing and assignment across mixed-vendor drones under human supervision; runs as an app on AuterionOS; whether allocation is centralised or distributed, and how it reassigns after losses, is not public — [Auterion](https://auterion.com/auterion-launches-nemyx-enabling-fully-coordinated-drone-swarms/); [Resilience Media](https://www.resiliencemedia.co/p/auterion-demonstrates-a-multi-manufacturer)
- Digital pheromones in the field (Sauter et al. 2008-2009): demonstration at NASA Wallops Island with multiple Aerosonde UAVs and Pioneer UGVs; one stigmergic algorithm self-organised surveillance, detection, cueing and recognition with no central processing; handled some unplanned hardware failures without human intervention; one operator could monitor and manage via an operator interface; supports mixed manned/unmanned teaming — [SoarTech SPIE 2008](https://soartech.com/knowledge_center/distributed-pheromone-based-swarming-control-of-unmanned-air-and-ground-vehicles-for-rsta/); [SoarTech 2009 IEEE HST](https://soartech.com/knowledge_center/demonstration-of-swarming-control-of-unmanned-ground-and-air-systems-in-surveillance-and-infrastructure-protection/)
- EPFL SMAVNET: ant-inspired, positionless fixed-wing MAV swarm to build communication relays for disaster response; evaluated in 2D/3D simulation (20 MAVs in the 2007 version); communication range vs turning rate identified as central for fixed-wing flocking — [SMAVNET project](https://lis2.epfl.ch/CompletedResearchProjects/SwarmingMAVs); [Hauert et al. 2008](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/124898/files/SI_final.pdf?version=1)
- EU SAGA (ECHORD++): UAV swarm weed mapping in sugar beet with random-walk/shared-visited-cell coverage; authors state swarm robotics research "is still confined into the lab"; field tests were scheduled for 2017, published field outcomes not found — [ERCIM News](https://ercim-news.ercim.eu/en113/special/drone-swarms-in-the-field); [WUR record (AVSS 2017)](https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/monitoring-and-mapping-with-robot-swarms-for-agricultural-applica/)
- EU Swarmanoid (FET-Open, 2006-2010): heterogeneous swarm of about 60 eye-bots (flying/ceiling-attaching), hand-bots (climbing) and foot-bots (ground); eye-bots gave local directions to foot-bots in navigation studies — [Swarmanoid project](https://www.swarmanoid.org/project_description.php.html); IEEE RAM 2013 paper DOI 10.1109/MRA.2013.2252996 — [EPFL record](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/handle/20.500.14299/79071)
- Kilobots (Rubenstein, Cornejo & Nagpal 2014, Science 345:795-799): 1,024 robots self-assembled 2D shapes using only local communication (about three body diameters), with algorithms tolerant of errors and stragglers — [Harvard SEAS](https://seas.harvard.edu/news/self-organizing-thousand-robot-swarm); [Princeton record](https://ssr.princeton.edu/publications/programmable-self-assembly-thousand-robot-swarm)
- Krieger et al. 2000: ant-like task allocation and recruitment validated on real (Khepera) robots — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/35023164)
- State of the field: "translating this research into practice represents a challenge that still needs to be tackled" — [Dorigo, Theraulaz & Trianni 2021, Proc IEEE 109(7)](https://comdig.unam.mx/2021/06/26/swarm-robotics-past-present-and-future); companion editorial 2020 in Science Robotics — [ULB record](https://difusion.ulb.ac.be/vufind/Record/ULB-DIPOT:oai:dipot.ulb.ac.be:2013/326716/TOC)

### Inferences
- What demonstrably worked outdoors: decentralised flocking with explicit modelling of delay and comms limits (Vásárhelyi), incremental scale-up with few operators supervising (ARSENL), stigmergic surveillance coordination tolerant of hardware faults (Sauter). Small multi-UAV search/monitoring teams of about three aircraft (Shield AI wildfire and search) match the proposed fleet size most closely.
- No public source documents a fielded UAV product using pure response-threshold or bee-quorum allocation; bio-inspired decision mechanisms remain mostly lab/robot-swarm validated (Kilobots, Khepera, e-puck-class robots).
- Common thread across fielded systems: human supervisors define objectives and approve, autonomy handles assignment and timing. This supports an operator-in-the-loop confirmation step as a legitimate "quorum member" rather than a departure from swarm principles.

### Gaps
- Allocation algorithms used in DARPA OFFSET, Perdix, Shield AI Hivemind and Auterion Nemyx are not publicly documented; no independent performance evaluation found.
- DARPA CODE not researched (military strike-focused; out of civilian scope).
- ETH Zurich swarm work and further EU projects (e.g. border, firefighting, Horizon swarm projects) were not covered in this pass.
- No peer-reviewed field trial of CBBA on fixed-wing UAVs was retrieved (MIT ACL indoor/outdoor tests exist per lab page but details not extracted).

## Lessons for a small civilian fleet (3-10 fixed-wing UAVs, operator in the loop)

### Takeaway
At this size, communication and full state sharing are affordable, so an acknowledged auction (or identical deterministic solve on a shared snapshot) is the right primary allocator for exclusive POI ownership. Social insect mechanisms are most valuable as the surrounding rules: decaying scout reports, independent confirmation (quorum) before commitment, targeted stop/abort signals, urgency-triggered backfill by reserves, and stigmergic coverage maps for search phases. All quorum and threshold parameters trade speed against accuracy and must be named, justified and tested.

### Cited Findings
- Market methods outperform thresholds when information is accurate; thresholds match quality at lower cost when information is inaccurate — [Kalra & Martinoli 2006](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/83462/files/NK_DARS06.pdf?version=2)
- CBBA guarantees (conflict-free, ≥50% optimal) depend on diminishing marginal gain scoring and, in the original proof, a connected network with synchronised rounds — [MIT ACL](https://acl.mit.edu/projects/consensus-based-bundle-algorithm); asynchronous lossy networks can yield duplicate or missing assignments — [SigPort](https://sigport.org/node/3370)
- Quorum threshold sets a speed-accuracy trade-off with diminishing accuracy gains above a point — [ScienceDaily on Passino & Seeley 2006](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2006/04/060420233908.htm)
- Cross-inhibiting stop signals prevent deadlock and split decisions but at high quorum can trap the group in local optima — [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/node/271186); [Laomettachit et al. 2016](https://kirim.kmutt.ac.th/converis/portal/detail/Publication/600978840?lang=en_GB)
- Peril-triggered negative feedback targeted at those committed to the endangered option reallocates labour efficiently — [Nieh 2010](https://labs.biology.ucsd.edu/nieh/papers/NiehCurrBio2010.pdf)
- Reserve workers backfill only when active workers are lost and the task is urgent — [Charbonneau et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0184074)
- Recruitment helps for clustered tasks but interference grows with group size — [Krieger et al. 2000](https://www.nature.com/articles/35023164)
- Design patterns exist to map bee-inspired macroscopic decision models to individual robot rules with quantitative predictions — [Reina et al. 2015](https://istc.cnr.it/en/node/75448)
- Fielded multi-UAV systems keep a few human supervisors over autonomous allocation — [US Navy ARSENL](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/Press-Releases/display-pressreleases/Article/2263000/arsenl-reaches-its-ultimate-goal-of-50-autonomous-uavs-in-flight/); [Auterion](https://auterion.com/auterion-launches-nemyx-enabling-fully-coordinated-drone-swarms/); [Shield AI 2022](https://shield.ai/hivemind-for-operational-read-and-react-swarming/)

### Inferences
- When to use which:
  - Auction (acked, single-assignment, deterministic tie-break by score then UAV ID): exclusive POI ownership (catcher assignment), any task where two UAVs acting at once is unsafe (approach to a dock, payload hand-off). Add ownership leases with heartbeat expiry so a dead winner's task returns to auction; re-auction only on explicit events (new report, lease expiry, abort) to avoid oscillation; use hysteresis (a challenger must beat the incumbent by a named margin) to stop churn.
  - Response thresholds: degraded-comms fallback and backup activation. Stimulus = age/urgency of an unserved, still-valid POI; fixed, distinct thresholds per backup produce a predictable activation order; still require an ownership claim broadcast so peers can stand down when they hear it.
  - Stigmergy: shared visited-cell/coverage or "evidence" maps for scouting, survey, spraying and fire-spot search (SAGA-style); not for exclusive assignment.
- Borrowing bee recruitment safely:
  - Scout report = dance: include observation time, confidence and a validity window; weight decays unless re-observed (dance attrition); never auction a POI whose report has expired.
  - Commitment requires a quorum appropriate to N: e.g. scout report plus an independent second detection (another UAV's camera) or operator confirmation before a catcher commits to irreversible actions. Keep quorum size a named, tested parameter; quorum must count independent observations, not re-broadcasts of the same report.
  - Stop signal = targeted abort/"POI invalid" message from any UAV or the operator, addressed to UAVs committed to that POI; it should suppress further recruitment for that POI (negative feedback) without stopping unrelated work.
  - Cross-inhibition is only needed when several candidate POIs compete for the same scarce catcher; with an auction, this is already handled by exclusive ownership.
- Determinism and verifiability: an auction over an acked, versioned snapshot is testable by replay and unit tests; threshold and stigmergic rules should be made deterministic (fixed thresholds, seeded or no randomness) and validated with simulation sweeps of message loss, peer death and stale reports. Simulation evidence supports allocation logic only; it says nothing about physical docking or cargo receipt.
- Failure-mode checklist to test in simulation: (1) winner dies after ack (lease expiry → re-auction); (2) partition: two subgroups each assign the same POI (ownership conflict resolution on merge by deterministic rule); (3) stale scout report recruits catcher to an empty location (decay/expiry); (4) all backups trigger at once (staggered thresholds); (5) oscillation between two near-equal catchers (hysteresis); (6) operator abort mid-commit (stop signal path).

### Gaps
- No source found that evaluates bee-style quorum or threshold allocation on small fixed-wing UAV fleets in flight; the guidance above is inferred from robot and insect studies.
- No published guidance found on certifying or formally verifying swarm task allocation for civilian airspace operations.
- Fixed-wing-specific constraints (minimum speed, loiter radius, inability to hover while waiting as backup) on allocation were not covered by any retrieved source beyond SMAVNET's communication-range vs turning-rate observation.
