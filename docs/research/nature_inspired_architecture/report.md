# Keep NavPy's layers, harden their boundaries

Yes, the proposed architecture has the right shape, and the nature analogy holds up. Animal nervous systems, the longest-running robot autonomy systems and today's drone stacks (PX4, ArduPilot, Nav2, Aerostack2) all arrived at the same split. An upper level picks a routine and sets its parameters, the routine below runs and corrects itself without being steered in detail, and a small, simple safety core underneath always wins. NASA flew a spacecraft this way for more than 12 years. The F-16's Auto-GCAS, a simple override that takes the controls only when the jet is about to hit the ground, is credited with saving at least eight pilots. The layers are fine; the work is at the points where they connect. Four questions need answers there: which reflexes are hard (the autopilot's) and which are soft (the companion's hold), how an override takes control and gives it back, what every skill must promise, and how the fleet keeps stale or duplicate tasks from sending two UAVs to one POI. Insect colonies supply good rules for the last question: reports fade unless refreshed, commitment waits for independent confirmation, a stop signal goes only to those involved, and reserves wake only when work goes undone. Even so, the acknowledged auction stays the right allocator for a fleet of 3-10 aircraft. Much of what the research supports is already in the October design drafts (`docs/design/mission_modules.md`, `component_ports.md`, `flow_runner.md`), so most of the changes below are additions or sharper rules. One limit: no published study tests this exact design on fixed-wing ArduPilot swarms. The advice is carried over from similar systems, and no simulation can show that a UAV physically docked or that cargo was received.

## Eight changes, in plain words

1. **Treat the autopilot's protections as the only hard reflexes.** Geofence, failsafes, Advanced Failsafe termination and the GUIDED command timeout keep working if the companion crashes. The companion's holds are soft reflexes and can never be the safety case. Don't copy autopilot failsafes onto the companion.
2. **Make override a filter that stays engaged.** Companion reflexes sit after the command arbiter and replace its output, and the most severe one wins. After one fires, nothing resumes on its own: the flow must hand control back to a skill explicitly. Every takeover is logged and counted.
3. **A skill may adjust a soft reflex only by declaring it in advance.** Example: a pickup dive may lower the companion's altitude floor within a fixed bound, logged, and only until that skill ends. Upper layers never adjust the hard reflexes.
4. **Write down a contract for every skill.** It states pre-conditions, hold-conditions checked every tick, outcomes (success, failure with reason, preempted), and the commands start, stop and modify. Results are things that can be observed, such as "approach passed", never "docked".
5. **Check sensor data before anything uses it.** Catch stale frames, frame-rate drops and impossible line-of-sight rates before any skill or approach law sees them.
6. **Make the world model a shared service, not a layer, and let attention follow the active skill.** One always-running store of the current state, which every layer reads. The gimbal points wherever the active skill asks, through the gimbal's single owner.
7. **Keep missions as graphs, with guards re-checked every tick.** Don't switch to behavior trees now. Keep Mission thin, and run each vehicle's loop on a single thread.
8. **Add insect rules around the auction.** Scout reports fade over time. Irreversible acts need a second, independent observation or the operator. An abort for a POI goes only to the UAVs working on it. Backups wake on urgency, in a fixed staggered order, when acknowledgements stop. A new bidder must beat the current owner by a set margin before taking a task over.

**Keep as drafted:**
- the four layers;
- a small set of Moves supplied by the autopilot plugin;
- approach laws as pure plugins that turn inputs into commands, with declared inputs, locked in at arming;
- one owner per actuator;
- ports with conformance kits;
- leases and fencing tokens for tasks only one UAV may hold.

When the AI scenario layer arrives, treat it like any other component whose code can't be checked in advance: it suggests at the Mission level, acts only through skills, and stays inside the same safety monitors.

## Brains delegate whole routines; colonies decide without a boss

In vertebrates each level owns a complete routine that corrects itself. The level above mostly chooses it, starts or stops it, and sets its speed or goal. Spinal circuits time the muscles and correct for bumps. Brainstem centres set how hard those circuits drive, and with that the walking speed ([Grillner & El Manira](https://insight.jci.org/references/scholar/3959/B30)). The basal ganglia act as a **central switch that decides which program gets the body**. Redgrave and colleagues argue a central switch beats spread-out alternatives whenever competing systems want the same limited resource ([Redgrave, Prescott & Gurney 1999](https://org.osu.edu/cognitive-science-club/files/2020/01/RedgravePrescottGurney99.pdf)). The cerebellum predicts the result of a command, which covers for slow feedback ([Wolpert, Miall & Kawato 1998](https://wolpertlab.neuroscience.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/content/papers/WolMiaKaw98.pdf)). The cortex sets how strongly each task corrects its errors rather than spelling out every movement ([Scott 2002](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~todorov/papers/ScottNatNeurosci02_news.pdf)). There are only a few basic building blocks. Stimulating two sites in a frog's spinal cord produced roughly the sum of the two separate force patterns in **83% of cases** ([Bizzi & Cheung 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2013.00051/full)). That supports a small set of Moves that each hold themselves stable, which Skills choose and set parameters for.

Two details matter for NavPy. First, the brain has no single "world model" box. Prediction sits in the cerebellum. A midbrain area, the superior colliculus, is the **final gatekeeper between attention and actual movement**, and it sits close to where actions are chosen, not in a distant planner ([PMC5206280](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206280); [Krauzlis et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-neuro-062012-170249)). Flies keep a heading estimate running at all times, and other circuits read it ([Pisokas, Heinze & Webb 2020](https://elifesciences.org/articles/53985)). Second, intention does not switch reflexes off. During voluntary movement the brain damps the sensory signals that drive spinal reflexes ([Seki, Perlmutter & Fetz 2003](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn1154)), and one leg reflex even reverses direction on stairs ([Lamont & Zehr 2006](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00221-006-0586-4)). Protection is always on, but its strength depends on the situation. That is the model for hard reflexes that never give way, plus soft ones a skill may adjust within limits.

Honeybee swarms pick a new nest site without a leader. Scouts dance for a site in proportion to its quality and dance less on each return. Support that is not renewed by new visits fades on its own ([American Bee Journal 2017](https://americanbeejournal.mydigitalpublication.com/june-2017-vol-157-no-6/page-622?pp=1)). The choice is not a vote of the whole swarm. It is made when a quorum of about **15-30 scouts is at one site at the same time** ([Seeley & Visscher 2004](https://bees.ucr.edu/media/166/download); [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/index%2ephp/node/276432)). A lower quorum is faster but makes more mistakes; raising it above 20-30 buys little extra accuracy ([Passino & Seeley 2006, via ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2006/04/060420233908.htm)). Scouts head-butt dancers promoting rival sites, a "stop signal" that breaks ties ([Cornell Chronicle 2011](https://news.cornell.edu/node/271186)). Foragers attacked at a food source make about **43 times** more stop signals, and they direct them only at nestmates visiting that same source ([Nieh 2010](https://labs.biology.ucsd.edu/nieh/papers/NiehCurrBio2010.pdf)).

Ants share out work through local cues. A worker takes on a task when the need for it passes that worker's own threshold, and doing the work reduces the need ([Lichocki et al. 2012, on Bonabeau's model](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/182822/files/lichocki12b.pdf?version=1)). Task choice also depends on how often an ant meets nestmates doing other jobs ([Gordon lab](https://web.stanford.edu/~dmgordon/old2/research.html)). In lab colonies of one species, about **40% of workers were mostly idle**. When the most active fifth was removed, these reserves took over within about a week, and how much they took on depended on how urgent the task was ([Charbonneau, Sasaki & Dornhaus 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0184074)). Pheromone trails coordinate ants through marks left in their surroundings instead of messages, an idea called stigmergy. UAV work has already copied it as "digital pheromones" on a shared map, which build up, spread to neighbours and fade over time ([Univ. Pisa](https://arpi.unipi.it/handle/11568/752393); [Hauert et al. 2008](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/124898/files/SI_final.pdf?version=1)). The match to NavPy is direct: the scout's report is the dance, the catcher is the recruited bee, and the backup is the reserve worker.

## Robots kept nature's reflexes but dropped its wiring

Rodney Brooks's subsumption design (1986) stacked fast reacting layers, and higher layers overrode lower ones by blocking their signals ([Brooks 1986](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1087032)). Its bottom layer worked well enough to ship in **more than 10 million Roombas** ([IEEE RAS](https://www.ieee-ras.org/about-ras/latest-news/congratulations-to-rodney-brooks-recipient-of-the-2023-ieee-founders-medal)). As a design for a whole system it hit a limit. Hartley and Pipitone found that upper layers reached into lower layers' internals, so the layers could not be designed separately. Even small changes to a low-level behavior or to the vehicle forced a redesign of the whole controller. They also observed that "sometimes the low level should override higher levels" ([Gat 1998](https://flownet.com/gat/papers/tla.pdf)). JPL's Tooth and Rocky III robots had upper layers that only passed advice and parameters downward, and they ran dozens of trials without failure (same source). Arkin's motor schemas added the behaviors' direction-and-speed outputs together instead of letting one override the others ([Arkin 1989](https://www.ini.rub.de/upload/file/1527093387_3a981e55d1876c5e1a88/Arkin89.pdf)). That blends smoothly, but it can leave the vehicle stuck or swinging back and forth ([Goodrich notes](https://phoenix.goucher.edu/~jillz/cs325_robotics/goodrich_potential_fields.pdf)). That risk is worse for a fixed-wing aircraft, which cannot stop.

The design that lasted is the **three-layer architecture**, which at least three groups arrived at independently. It has a fast controller, a sequencer that chooses the active behavior and sets its parameters, and a slow planner ([Gat 1998](https://flownet.com/gat/papers/tla.pdf)). Gat sorts code by memory, which gives NavPy a test for where new code goes. Code with no memory goes in the controller, code that remembers the past goes in the sequencer, and code that predicts goes in the planner. This design has long real-world records:
- NASA's 3T ran a water-recovery life-support system on its own, around the clock, for **16 months** ([Bonasso et al. 2003](https://traclabs.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/ai_mag2003.pdf)).
- The Autonomous Sciencecraft software flew NASA's EO-1 satellite for **more than 12 years**, with the flight software's own independent fault protection running beneath it ([Sherwood et al. 2006](https://ai.jpl.nasa.gov/public/papers/sherwood-spaceops06-autonomy.pdf); [Geology In 2017](https://www.geologyin.com/2017/03/how-ai-captured-volcanos-changing-lava.html)).

Failures cluster in the middle layer:
- **Deep Space 1 (1999).** Two threads of the Remote Agent software deadlocked because a lock was missing, and it failed to stop the ion engine. The race had never shown up in thousands of ground runs, yet an automated proof tool (model checking) had found five similar threading bugs before launch ([Nayak et al. 1999](https://ai.jpl.nasa.gov/public/documents/papers/rax-results-isairas99.pdf); [Havelund et al.](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20000055731/downloads/20000055731.pdf)).
- **CLARAty (JPL).** Its designers merged planner and executive partly because separate planners kept their own copy of the robot's state ([Nesnas et al. 2006](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.5772/5766)). That is a warning against a Mission layer that is too separate and against duplicate world models.

Two other approaches are not worth a small team's time. Action selectors modelled on the brain have only run on small lab robots ([Prescott et al. 2006](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/107037)). A survey of 84 cognitive architectures notes a gap between them and mainstream robotics ([Kotseruba & Tsotsos](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08602v2)).

Aviation turned "reflexes win" into a formal pattern called Simplex, or run-time assurance. A complex controller runs freely. A small monitor that can be fully checked hands control to a simple recovery controller whenever the vehicle nears a safety limit ([Sha, via Purdue slides](https://engineering.purdue.edu/dcsl/reading/2007/foob-using_simplicity_to_control_complexity.pdf); [Hobbs et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03506)). Auto-GCAS on the F-16 works this way. It warns the pilot, then blocks the pilot's inputs, rolls the jet upright and pulls up until the terrain is clear, then gives control back. It was credited with **eight pilots saved in about 4.5 years** across 600+ jets ([NASA 2019](https://www.nasa.gov/centers/armstrong/news/newsreleases/2019/19-02.html); [The Aviationist](https://theaviationist.com/2015/02/02/f-16-gcat-explained/)). The drone standard ASTM F3269 names the parts: an input checker, a monitor, a recovery function and a switch. It treats repeated recoveries in one mission as a sign that the complex function is faulty ([ASTM F3269-21](https://store.astm.org/f3269-21.html)). ArduPilot already has the lowest level of this pattern. Advanced Failsafe terminates the flight on a fence breach, and the flight controller's backup processor terminates it if the main processor stops responding ([ArduPilot AFS](https://ardupilot.org/plane/docs/advanced-failsafe-configuration.html)). NASA's ICAROUS is the closest published match to NavPy's split: monitors on a companion computer work out recovery manoeuvres, and the autopilot flies them ([NASA LaRC](https://shemesh.larc.nasa.gov/fm/ICAROUS)).

Outdoor swarm successes are mostly about flying together and teams under human supervision, not insect-style task allocation:
- Thirty drones flocked outdoors without central control, but only after radio delays and range limits were modelled explicitly ([Vásárhelyi et al. 2018](https://hal.elte.hu/drones/scirob2018.html)).
- The Naval Postgraduate School flew **50 fixed-wing UAVs with two operators**, building up from 6 aircraft over about nine months ([US Navy](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/Press-Releases/display-pressreleases/Article/2263000/arsenl-reaches-its-ultimate-goal-of-50-autonomous-uavs-in-flight/)).
- A digital-pheromone demonstration with aircraft and ground robots coordinated surveillance with no central computer and kept working through some hardware failures ([SoarTech 2008](https://soartech.com/knowledge_center/distributed-pheromone-based-swarming-control-of-unmanned-air-and-ground-vehicles-for-rsta/)).

No public UAV product in service uses bee-quorum or threshold allocation. Leading authors still call moving swarm research into practice an open challenge ([Dorigo, Theraulaz & Trianni 2021](https://comdig.unam.mx/2021/06/26/swarm-robotics-past-present-and-future)).

On allocation itself:
- **Consensus auctions (CBAA/CBBA).** They give assignments with no conflicts that score **at least 50% of the best possible**, but only on a fully connected network with bidding rounds kept in step ([MIT ACL](https://acl.mit.edu/projects/consensus-based-bundle-algorithm)). On lossy, out-of-step links, one study saw a task given to two UAVs or to none (slides, not peer reviewed) ([Rantanen et al., via SigPort](https://sigport.org/node/3370)).
- **Auctions versus thresholds.** Auctions do better when information is accurate. When it isn't, thresholds do as well at a fraction of the cost ([Kalra & Martinoli 2006](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/83462/files/NK_DARS06.pdf?version=2)).
- **Recruiting help.** It pays off when work is clustered, but robots get in each other's way as the group grows ([Krieger et al. 2000](https://www.nature.com/articles/35023164)).

## PX4, ArduPilot and Nav2 already draw the same lines

| NavPy layer | In nature | In robots and drone stacks | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mission | Cortex sets goals | Planner; Nav2 tree navigator; PX4 mode executor; MOOS-IvP modes | Keep it thin |
| Skills | Basal ganglia choose a routine and set its parameters | Sequencer; Aerostack2 behaviors; SkiROS2 skills | Keep; add a contract |
| Moves | Spinal programs and force-pattern building blocks | Controller; PX4 setpoint types; ArduPilot modes and GUIDED | Keep the set small |
| Reflexes | Always-on spinal reflexes, adjusted by situation | Simplex; PX4 and ArduPilot failsafes; Nav2 Collision Monitor | Split hard and soft |
| Senses | Attention gate; heading estimator | Perception plugins; F3269 input checker | Add an input check |
| Nerves | Brain-to-spine pathways | Nav2 health heartbeats; GUIDED timeout; peer heartbeats | Each side checks the other is alive |
| Colony | Scouts, recruits, reserves, quorum | Auctions, thresholds, digital pheromones | Auction plus insect rules |

The stacks get specific about how overrides work:
- **PX4** lets companion code register its own flight modes, with three limits. A crashed or unresponsive external mode triggers a failsafe. A mission executor loses control the moment the pilot switches mode or a failsafe fires. A mode may postpone only non-essential failsafes, for example to finish a winch operation ([PX4 ROS 2 Control Interface](https://docs.px4.io/main/en/ros2/px4_ros2_control_interface)). When several failsafes fire, **the most severe action wins** ([PX4 Safety](https://docs.px4.io/main/en/config/safety)).
- **ArduPilot** takes a stream of GUIDED setpoints from the companion and watches it. In the Copter documentation the vehicle stops after 3 s without new commands ([ArduPilot Guided](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/ac2_guidedmode.html)). Mode changes made by a failsafe stay in place until the pilot changes mode ([ArduPilot failsafes](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/failsafe-landing-page.html)). Lua scripts suit small monitors on the autopilot that survive a companion crash. They do not suit approach laws, because scripts are not guaranteed to run on time and all of them stop if one crashes ([ArduPilot Lua](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/common-lua-scripts.html)).
- **Nav2**, the ROS 2 navigation stack for ground robots, places its Collision Monitor as a filter between the controller and the motors. It works on raw sensor data and applies the most aggressive zone that has triggered. Recoveries are ordinary branches of the mission tree, kept separate from that reflex ([Nav2 Collision Monitor](https://docs.nav2.org/tutorials/docs/using_collision_monitor.html)).
- **Aerostack2** gives each behavior start, pause, resume and stop, plus a "modify" call that changes parameters without stopping. Its real multi-drone flights used only two aircraft ([Aerostack2](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.18237v2)).
- **MOOS-IvP**, autonomy software for underwater vehicles that runs on a separate computer behind the vehicle's own autopilot, takes the opposite approach. Each cycle it blends all active behaviors by weighing their preferences ([MOOS-IvP helm](https://oceanai.mit.edu/moos-ivp-pdf/moosivp-helm-13.2.pdf); [GD Mission Systems](https://gdmissionsystems.com/articles/2011/07/15/moos-ivp-the-open-source-backseat-driver-software-successfully-demonstrated-on-bluefin-9)). That is a credible later option for soft concerns such as keeping distance from peers.

## Where each open question lands

| Question | Recommendation | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Where does the world model live? | A shared, always-running state service: own vehicle, tracks and fleet picture. Every layer reads it; only the parts that produce each piece of data write it. Layers keep their own memory rules: reflexes keep almost no memory, skills remember the past, Mission predicts. The approach law still receives only its declared inputs, so the shared model cannot pass compass yaw, ground speed or altitude to it. | Cerebellum and the fly heading estimate work as services; CLARAty's duplicate-state problem ([Nesnas](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.5772/5766)) |
| Where does attention live? | With the active skill, granted by the gimbal's single owner. Background services borrow the camera at lower priority. The gimbal is both a sense and an actuator: its pointing goes through its owner, and its angle readings count only as camera state. | Superior colliculus as the gatekeeper next to action choice ([PMC5206280](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206280)) |
| Where does arbitration live? | One arbiter per actuator in each vehicle's executive, with the reflex filter after it. One skill at a time, never blended. It gives way to danger fast and resumes slowly. Consider weighted blending only later, and only for soft concerns. | Basal ganglia ([Redgrave](https://org.osu.edu/cognitive-science-club/files/2020/01/RedgravePrescottGurney99.pdf)); subsumption's failure and Alfred's slow resume ([Gat](https://flownet.com/gat/papers/tla.pdf)) |
| Graphs or behavior trees? | Graphs (state machines), with hold-condition guards re-checked every tick, which is the main thing behavior trees offer. Reconsider trees only if graphs fill up with copied fallback edges. | Projects use trees and state machines in similar ways ([Ghzouli 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04211)); state machines grow badly at large scale ([Iovino 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05842)) |
| What must a skill promise? | Pre-conditions; hold-conditions; outcomes (success, failure with reason, preempted); start, stop and modify; observable results; it reports its own failures; it re-reads the actual autopilot mode every tick. | [SkiROS2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17030); [Aerostack2](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.18237v2); Deep Space 1's mismatch between software belief and spacecraft state ([Nayak](https://ai.jpl.nasa.gov/public/documents/papers/rax-results-isairas99.pdf)) |
| How is reflex override enforced? | Hard reflexes on the autopilot. Companion reflexes act as a filter that switches cleanly to tested Moves (loiter, RTL, land). The override stays engaged until a skill is explicitly handed control again. Postponing is narrow and declared in advance. Every activation is counted. | [Simplex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03506); [F3269](https://store.astm.org/f3269-21.html); [PX4](https://docs.px4.io/main/en/ros2/px4_ros2_control_interface); [ArduPilot](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/failsafe-landing-page.html) |
| What is decided locally and what globally? | Each UAV decides how to fly, its own safety and its approach. The fleet decides which UAV owns which exclusive task and shares coverage. The operator sets goals and confirms irreversible acts. | Bee quorum is local ([Seeley & Visscher](https://bees.ucr.edu/media/166/download)); every fielded programme keeps human supervisors ([US Navy](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/Press-Releases/display-pressreleases/Article/2263000/arsenl-reaches-its-ultimate-goal-of-50-autonomous-uavs-in-flight/)) |
| How should the swarm share out tasks? | An acknowledged single-task auction with leases, a fixed tie-break, re-auction only when something happens, and a margin before a new bidder takes over. Thresholds are the fallback when links degrade. A shared coverage map serves search-type modules. At 10 UAVs or fewer, an alternative is for every UAV to run the same solver on the same acknowledged snapshot. | [CBBA](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/52330); [Kalra & Martinoli](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/83462/files/NK_DARS06.pdf?version=2); reserve ants ([Charbonneau](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0184074)); [SAGA](https://ercim-news.ercim.eu/en113/special/drone-swarms-in-the-field) |

The bee quorum needs scaling down. In a fleet of 3-10 the quorum can only be tiny: the scout's report plus one independent observation, from another UAV's camera or from the operator. It must count separate observations, never the same report passed on again. Its size is a named, tested setting, because the bees show it trades speed for accuracy. Backups on fixed-wing aircraft cannot hover while they wait, since "hold" means loitering in an orbit. Waiting therefore costs energy, which is one more reason to wake backups only when a task goes unserved, as ant reserves do.

## What the evidence cannot settle yet

No source evaluates layered autonomy on small fixed-wing fleets flying ArduPilot with a companion computer. The nearest cases are ICAROUS and Linköping's WITAS helicopter work ([WITAS](https://www.ida.liu.se/ext/witas/info/arch/display.html)). No retrieved source covers fixed-wing limits such as minimum airspeed, turn radius and no hover, either for designing recovery manoeuvres or for loitering backups. That needs aerodynamics and ArduPilot specialist input. The ArduPilot timeout and latching details above come from the Copter documentation. No fetched page says whether a fence breach overrides companion GUIDED commands, or how failsafes rank against each other, so check ArduPlane behavior in SITL before relying on it. The finding that auctions break on lossy links rests on conference slides, and the bee quorum numbers come from secondary accounts. The allocation methods of the large military swarm programmes are not public. Simulation can show that allocation logic is correct; it never shows physical docking or cargo receipt.

## Conclusion

The past systems failed where parts met, not in their layers. Subsumption's override wiring, Remote Agent's executive threads and planners that kept stale copies of the robot's state all failed at the connections. NavPy's design already has the right parts. The next step is to write a contract for each connection and test it against six failures before adding any intelligence:
- the winning bidder dies;
- the network splits;
- a stale scout report;
- every backup wakes at once;
- two catchers keep swapping a task;
- the operator aborts mid-task.

Biology also changes how module pressure to relax safety limits should be handled, as with pickup dives or fire suppression flying low. Reflexes in animals are adjusted, not just triggered. That gives a principled answer: declare the change, bound it, log it, end it with the skill, and never touch the autopilot's hard limits. The same thinking makes the future AI layer one more complex component in the Simplex sense. The stronger the monitors and contracts built now, the more freedom a model can safely be given later.
